## Executive view

1. physics training centered on condensed matter and superconductivity;
2. substantial software-development and consulting experience;
3. quantum-software work attached to experimental dual-rail cavity-qubit systems;
4. continued work with the New Haven gate-model organization after Quantum Circuits joined D-Wave.

The most defensible claim about his current technical depth is narrow:

> **Source-backed fact:** Luke is a coauthor on the 2024 dual-rail measurement paper and the 2026 dual-rail entangling-gate paper. An open preprint for the entangling-gate work explicitly credits him and three colleagues with developing software used for the project. [P1][P2][P3]

That supports direct familiarity with software serving superconducting dual-rail experiments. It does **not** prove that he personally designed the qubit, calibrated the SWS gate, operated every instrument, authored every analysis, or owns every layer of D-Wave's private stack.

## Evidence language

- **Source-backed fact:** directly stated by a primary source, paper, official posting, or first-party company page.
- **Corroborated public background:** supported by more than one public biographical record, but not independently verified from an institutional transcript or employment record.
- **Provisional inference:** a reasonable interview model, not a fact about Luke's preferences or private work.
- **Unresolved question:** public evidence does not settle it.
- **Simulation boundary:** dialogue attributed to Luke in the companion conversation file is invented practice material, never a quotation.

## What he can credibly probe in an interview

These are **provisional inferences**, not predictions of his questions.

### Physics-to-software translation

He can reasonably ask whether a candidate understands that a software value attaches to a physical mode, pulse, classifier, device state, and validity interval. A generic database answer is unlikely to be enough.

### Error semantics

He can reasonably distinguish:

- erasure from an unknown Pauli error;
- detection from correction;
- leakage from an erasure reported to a decoder;
- postselection from real-time fault handling;
- average fidelity from a structured error channel.

### Experiment software as an instrument

His public papers make it plausible that he will care about whether software can change a scientific conclusion through assignment rules, shot filtering, timestamps, calibration selection, or schema drift.

### Productionization

His software history makes it plausible that he will ask how exploratory scientific code becomes a typed, tested, observable, reviewable system without blocking physicists from changing protocols.

### Evidence and debugging

The repeated-CZ result gives him a concrete way to test whether a candidate separates observation, hypothesis, diagnostic intervention, and conclusion.

### Honest scope control

Because his own path crosses domains, he may value a candidate who can state a hardware boundary precisely and still reason usefully about the software consequences.
