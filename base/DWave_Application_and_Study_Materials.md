#### Dual rail from first principles

###### One logical qubit = one excitation shared across two cavity modes

###### |1L> = |0,1>

###### |0,0>

photon in rail A

Known location/time turns a dominant unknown-looking fault into an erasure; residual phase/bit-flip errors still matter.

##### Encoding

Write the occupation of cavity A and cavity B as |nA,nB>. The logical code space uses one photon total: |0L> = |1,0> and |1L> = |0,1>. A general qubit is a coherent superposition α|1,0> + β|0,1>. The information is in which rail holds the excitation and their relative phase. [S5]

##### Why loss becomes an erasure

- The dominant cavity fault is single-photon loss. Either logical basis state then falls to |0,0>, outside the one-photon code space.

- A check can report ‘this qubit leaked here’ without learning α and β when no loss occurred. The decoder receives a known spacetime location rather than an unknown Pauli fault.

- Known locations are easier for a higher-level code to handle; that is the source of lower overhead—not an absence of errors.

##### Three distinctions interviewers notice

| Term | Correct distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Detection vs correction | Detection flags the event. Correction additionally resets/replaces the state and uses a code/decoder to<br>reconstruct protected information. |
| Erasure vs postselection | Postselection discards failed shots; fault-tolerant operation needs real-time handling, reset, syndrome logic,<br>and decoding—not endless discarding. |
| Erasure vs leakage | Leakage is leaving the computational subspace. When reliably detected and localized, it can be represented to<br>the code as an erasure. |

###### Metric memory aid

2024: logical SPAM error near 0.01%, over 99% of cavity-decay events detected, decay about 0.2%/μs, phase errors ~6× less frequent, bit flips at least 150× less frequent. Use these to understand the hierarchy; do not lead with a number dump. [S1]

###### SUPERCONDUCTING CIRCUIT QED

#### The device-physics stack you need—not a physics PhD

| Layer | Mental model | Why software cares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Superconductivity | Below a critical regime, electrons form Cooper pairs and<br>current can flow with negligible DC resistance; fields<br>and materials still produce loss/noise. | Temperature, magnetic environment, materials, and fabrication<br>change coherence and calibration. |
| Josephson junction | A nonlinear, non-dissipative circuit element. Its<br>Josephson inductance supplies the nonlinearity that lets<br>circuits behave as artificial atoms. | Flux/bias and pulse settings determine frequencies and interactions;<br>constraints are device-specific. |
| Transmon / SQUID<br>coupler | A weakly anharmonic superconducting mode used for<br>control, measurement ancilla, or tunable coupling. | T1/T2, flux response, leakage, and frequency drift affect pulse<br>validity. |
| Microwave cavity | A high-Q resonator holding quantized microwave<br>photon states; here, pairs of cavities store dual-rail<br>logical information. | Mode frequency, lifetime, cross-Kerr/dispersive shifts, and coupling<br>must be characterized. |
| Dispersive interaction | Two modes do not exchange excitations, but the<br>frequency/phase of one depends on the occupation of<br>the other. | Enables conditional phase and readout; fit χ and control phase<br>accumulation accurately. |
| Parametric beamsplitter | A pump activates excitation exchange between<br>selected modes, implementing rail rotations or swaps. | Frequency, amplitude, phase, duration, mixer response, and crosstalk<br>become calibration parameters. |
| Readout chain | Microwave response travels through cryogenic and<br>room-temperature amplifiers/mixers to digitizers,<br>producing IQ data. | Thresholds, classifiers, gain/phase drift, acquisition timing, and<br>metadata determine assignment. |

##### The minimum Hamiltonian language

You do not need to derive every circuit. Recognize two patterns: a beamsplitter interaction H/ħ ∝ g(a†b + ab†) swaps an excitation between modes; a dispersive/cross-Kerr term H/ħ ∝ χ n₁n₂ makes phase depend on occupation. Control software selects pump frequency, amplitude, phase, and duration so the intended effective Hamiltonian dominates. [S2][S6][S7]

###### Important nuance about Luke’s PhD

High-temperature cuprate superconductivity and low-temperature circuit-QED devices share quantum condensed-matter concepts, cryogenic experimentation, materials sensitivity, and careful inference. They are not interchangeable specialties. Treat his path as evidence that he learned to cross domains—not as permission to equate Pr-Ba-Cu-O phase physics with a cavity/transmon processor.

###### SINGLE-QUBIT CONTROL AND SWS CZ

#### From microwave pulses to an entangling gate

##### Single-qubit rotations

Within a dual rail, a parametrically driven beamsplitter coherently moves the photon between the two cavities. Pulse area sets rotation angle; drive phase sets rotation axis. The 2026 paper reports ~200 ns single-qubit gates, ~0.1% erasure, and ~0.01% postselected randomized-benchmarking error on the demonstrated system. [S2]

##### Swap–Wait–Swap controlled-Z

1. Swap: move the control rail’s excitation into a transmon coupler with a parametric sideband interaction.

2. Wait: the occupied coupler experiences a strong dispersive interaction with a target cavity. Waiting approximately π/χ accumulates a conditional π phase.

3. Swap back: return the excitation to the control cavity, ideally preserving the dual-rail code space.

| Property in the Aug. 2026 paper | Engineering consequence |
| --- | --- |
| ~500 ns gate; erasure ~0.5%; residual Pauli <0.1% | Optimize multiple metrics, not a single ‘fidelity’ number. |
| Bit flips at roughly 10⁻⁶ level | Noise is highly structured; decoder and tests should preserve category<br>information. |
| Control erasure ~0.400%; target ~0.096% | Gate orientation and scheduling may matter; asymmetric error models cannot<br>be flattened casually. |
| Loss during gate produces conditional dephasing | Leakage propagation semantics belong in simulator/compiler/decoder contracts. |
| Long sequences show nonlinear degradation | Calibration validity and drift monitoring can dominate beyond short benchmark<br>sequences. |

###### The sentence worth remembering

A gate is good not only when its average error is low, but when it preserves the favorable error hierarchy that the error-correcting code expects. Hardware, calibration, scheduling, simulation, and decoding must agree on that structure. [S2]

##### Terms to recognize

- QST: quantum state tomography reconstructs a state from many measurement bases.

- RB / IRB: randomized benchmarking estimates average gate error; interleaved RB isolates a target gate.

- SPAM: state-preparation and measurement error.

- Postselected fraction: fraction of shots remaining after detected erasures are removed.

##### Production invariants worth asking about

- Every value has units, device identity, source experiment, timestamp, code version, fit method, uncertainty, validity range, and parent dependencies.

- A parent change invalidates descendants deterministically; concurrent calibrations cannot silently overwrite one another.

- Promotion is atomic and reviewable; failed validation leaves the last-known-good state intact; rollback is boring and fast.

- Long-running jobs detect hardware-state changes and avoid mixing incompatible data into one fit.

- Raw data remain immutable; derived artifacts can be regenerated; dashboards expose both scientific metrics and service health.

#### Glossary: the vocabulary behind the conversation

Anharmonicity — Unequal oscillator level spacing that lets one transition act as a qubit without exciting higher levels.

IRB — Interleaved randomized benchmarking; estimates error of a target gate relative to a reference.

Beamsplitter interaction — Excitation-exchange coupling between two modes; controls rail rotations/swaps.

Josephson junction — Nonlinear superconducting element underlying transmon/coupler circuits.

cQED — Circuit quantum electrodynamics: superconducting artificial atoms coupled to microwave resonators.

Leakage — Leaving the computational codespace; detected leakage can become an erasure.

Coherence — Persistence of amplitude and phase information; commonly summarized by T1 and T2.

No-jump backaction — Conditional evolution when no decay is observed; absence of a jump still carries dynamical effect.

Codespace — Physical-state subspace used to represent valid logical states.

Parametric drive — Time-varying pump that activates/selects an effective interaction.

Coupler — Nonlinear/tunable element used to mediate interactions between modes.

Pauli error — Logical X, Y, or Z error whose location is generally not known directly.

CZ — Controlled-Z entangling gate; adds a phase to one joint basis state.

Postselection — Discarding runs based on a condition such as detected erasure; useful experimentally, insufficient alone for FTQC.

Dephasing / Z error — Loss or corruption of relative phase without necessarily exchanging logical populations.

QST — Quantum state tomography; reconstructs a state from measurements in multiple bases.

Dispersive shift χ — Occupation-dependent frequency shift enabling conditional phase and readout.

RB — Randomized benchmarking; estimates average gate performance using randomized sequences.

DRAG — Pulse-shaping method often used to reduce leakage in weakly anharmonic qubits.

SPAM — State-preparation-and-measurement errors.

T1 / T2 / Tφ — Energy-relaxation time / total coherence time / pure-dephasing time.

Erasure — Error with known location (and often time); easier for many QEC codes than an unknown Pauli error.

Transmon — Josephson-junction-based superconducting artificial atom engineered to reduce charge-noise sensitivity.

Fock state — State with a definite photon/excitation number, written |n>.

IQ data — In-phase and quadrature components of a measured microwave signal.

**BEGIN WITH THE TRANSLATION**

#### The entire question in ordinary language

###### One-sentence translation

When the team makes the same two-qubit operation run many times, the machine gets worse faster than a simple error-per-operation model predicts. The researchers suspect either that the software is still using old tuning numbers or that part of the physical machine is moving while the experiment runs. You are asking how their software records which tuning numbers are trustworthy, what those numbers depend on, and what measurements let them identify the real cause.

##### An everyday analogy: a piano and an automatic player

- A piano key is supposed to produce a particular pitch. A technician measures and tunes it. That measured tuning is like a calibration parameter.

- An automatic player stores instructions based on that pitch. The stored instruction is part of the software state.

- If the piano was retuned but the player still uses yesterday's instruction, the parameter is stale.

- If the piano's pitch is continuously wandering as temperature changes, the physical device is changing.

- Both problems sound wrong. They require different fixes and different evidence.

##### The five hidden questions

1. What exactly was measured?

2. What pattern did the measurement reveal?

3. Which possible causes are being considered, and how certain are they?

4. How does the software remember what the machine believes to be true?

5. How does the team experimentally distinguish old information from moving hardware?

###### What this question is not

It is not merely asking, 'Do you store calibration values in a database?' It is asking whether the software, experiments, and physical diagnostics form a coherent system that can explain why the machine stopped behaving as expected.

**THE OBJECT BEING REPEATED**

#### Start at zero: what is a quantum gate?

##### A qubit

A conventional bit stores 0 or 1. A qubit can occupy a quantum combination of two logical possibilities. Operations must change that state in a controlled way without accidentally destroying the information.

##### A gate

A quantum gate is a precisely controlled physical operation that transforms one or more qubits. In superconducting hardware, a gate is not an abstract box floating in space. It is realized by shaped microwave and flux-control signals applied for carefully chosen frequencies, amplitudes, phases, and durations.

##### A CZ gate

CZ means controlled-Z. It is a two-qubit entangling operation. In simplified language, it gives one joint logical condition a phase change while leaving the other computational conditions unchanged. Entangling gates are necessary because independent one-qubit operations cannot create general multi-qubit quantum behavior.

| Abstract description | Physical implementation question |
| --- | --- |
| Apply CZ | Which modes interact, through which coupler, for how long? |
| Add a phase | What interaction strength and wait time produce the intended phase? |
| Preserve the state | How much loss, dephasing, leakage, and crosstalk occurred? |
| Repeat reliably | Are the same parameter values still correct minutes or hours later? |

###### Why software belongs here

The command 'CZ' is the top of a chain. Software must translate it into device-specific pulse settings, schedule resources, capture measurements, attach the correct calibration version, and decide whether the result is still valid.

**CAVITIES, A COUPLER, AND A TUNED INTERACTION**

##### Dual-rail cavity qubits

In the architecture discussed by the D-Wave paper, one logical qubit is encoded using a single microwave excitation shared between two superconducting cavities. The cavities are long-lived resonant structures: like very precise containers for microwave energy, each with a natural frequency.

##### The coupler

A coupler is an intermediate superconducting element used to create a controlled interaction between parts of the processor. In the reported Swap-Wait-Swap CZ gate, an excitation is temporarily moved into a transmon coupler, allowed to acquire a conditional phase through a dispersive interaction, and then moved back. [1]

##### Why frequency matters

- Every resonant component has a natural frequency - its preferred 'pitch.'

- Control pulses are tuned relative to those frequencies. If the frequencies shift, the pulse can become mistuned.

- Mistuning can make a swap incomplete, rotate phase incorrectly, or introduce extra error.

- The coupler is especially relevant because it is temporarily occupied during the gate and its coherence and frequency influence the control qubit's error.

###### Recruiter-level version

The coupler is a controllable bridge. The software sends a signal tuned to that bridge. If the bridge's resonant 'pitch' changes, yesterday's perfect signal may be wrong today.

**THE STRESS TEST**

#### What does 'repeated-CZ data' mean?

##### One gate is not enough evidence

A single gate can look excellent even when it contains a small systematic mistake. That mistake may be too small to see once. Repeating the gate many times amplifies the effect, just as copying a slightly tilted line many times makes the tilt obvious.

##### The paper's experiment

1. Prepare two dual-rail qubits in a known state.

2. Apply a sequence containing an odd number of CZ gates so the ideal result is a known entangled state.

3. Repeat the experiment for different gate counts, reaching as many as 103 CZ gates.

4. Measure state fidelity, purity, and the fraction of shots that survive erasure postselection.

5. Compare the observed trend with the behavior expected from a simple, stable error-per-gate model.

**QUALITY GETS WORSE**

#### What does 'degradation' mean?

##### Degradation is a direction, not a diagnosis

To degrade means to lose desired quality. In this experiment, important output measures such as fidelity and purity decreased as more CZ gates were applied. That tells us performance worsened. It does not, by itself, say why.

| Metric | Plain meaning | What a decrease suggests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fidelity | How close the measured state is to the intended state. | The final quantum state is increasingly wrong. |
| Purity | How much the state resembles one well-defined quantum<br>state rather than a statistical mixture. | Noise or uncontrolled variation is mixing the result. |
| Postselected fraction | How many runs remain after detected erasures are<br>removed. | More runs are suffering detectable loss. |

##### Three kinds of worsening

- Random error: each gate adds independent noise; average quality often follows a simple exponential or nearly linear short-depth trend.

- Coherent systematic error: the same small over-rotation or phase error repeats and can build predictably.

- Time-varying error: the physical system or its controls change while measurements are averaged, mixing incompatible conditions.

###### The hidden engineering requirement

A production system should not reduce all three patterns to one number called 'gate error.' Their causes, operational risks, and corrective actions are different.

##### A useful follow-up

'Which metric first indicated that this was not ordinary independent error - fidelity, purity, erasure fraction, or a device monitor?' This asks how the team recognized the pattern, not just what number they reported.

##### Linear, in ordinary language

If every extra gate removed the same amount of quality, the graph would fall at a constant rate. Add ten more gates, lose roughly the same additional amount each time. That is a simple linear picture.

##### Nonlinear, in ordinary language

Nonlinear means the rate of worsening itself changes. The 70th added gate may expose more loss than the 10th added gate. A quadratic-looking trend bends downward because the total penalty grows roughly with the square of sequence depth, not merely in direct proportion to it.

##### Why that shape suggests deeper structure

- A parameter may be drifting during the many measurements used to estimate each point.

- A coherent phase error may accumulate rather than average away.

- The system may warm, saturate, or respond differently at large sequence depth.

- A model or analysis assumption that works at short depth may fail at long depth.

###### Scientific caution

A nonlinear curve narrows the list of plausible explanations, but the curve alone cannot identify the cause. Different mechanisms can produce similar shapes.

### Source page 11 of 22

**A ONCE-CORRECT RELATIONSHIP STOPS MATCHING**

#### What is calibration drift?

##### Drift

Drift is a slow or systematic change over time. Imagine measuring the correct radio station at 100.0 MHz in the morning, but the receiver's effective center moves to 100.1 MHz by afternoon. The old setting was valid when measured; it no longer matches the present system.

##### What can drift

- The physical device's resonance or coupling strength.

- Room-temperature or cryogenic electronics gain, phase, timing, or reference frequency.

- Readout distributions and classification thresholds.

- The relationship between a commanded value and the delivered waveform.

- Environmental conditions such as temperature, flux, vibration, or electromagnetic interference.

##### Drift is defined relative to a timescale

| Timescale | Possible symptom | Possible response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Across weeks | periodic recalibration gradually moves | scheduled qualification and trend monitoring |
| Across hours | morning calibration fails later | validity window, canaries, automatic refresh |
| Across minutes | long experiment mixes different states | interleaved monitors, segmentation, abort/retry |
| Across shots | broad or multi-modal result | real-time estimation, device investigation, robust analysis |

###### Important distinction

'Calibration drift' can mean the stored calibration becomes wrong because something underneath changed. It names a mismatch over time; it does not yet tell you whether the root cause is the qubit, coupler, control electronics, readout chain, environment, or software lineage.

### Source page 12 of 22

**THE PHYSICAL TARGET MAY BE MOVING**

#### What is coupler-frequency fluctuation?

##### Frequency

Frequency is how many oscillations occur per second. A resonant superconducting element responds strongly near particular microwave frequencies. Control pulses rely on knowing those frequencies accurately.

##### Fluctuation

A fluctuation is variation around a value. It may be slow, fast, random, periodic, or correlated with another condition. The coupler might not have one fixed frequency during the full data-collection interval.

##### How fluctuation creates gate error

1. The software chooses a pulse frequency and wait time based on a measured coupler frequency.

2. The physical coupler frequency moves away from that measured value.

3. The intended swap or conditional phase is no longer exact.

4. Repeating the imperfect gate accumulates phase error or produces varying results.

5. Averaging shots collected under different coupler frequencies can reduce purity and create nonlinear trends.

##### Why the coupler is a plausible candidate

The reported gate temporarily populates the coupler. The paper observes larger loss and dephasing on the control side, consistent with the coupler's lower coherence relative to the cavities, and notes nonlinear behavior in control-qubit Z errors. These observations motivate attention to the coupler, but still do not prove a specific fluctuation mechanism. [1]

###### Recruiter-level version

The software may have the right number for a machine that will not hold still. If the bridge's pitch moves during the experiment, continuously reusing one stored pitch will not solve the problem.

**DESIGN MEASUREMENTS THAT SEPARATE HYPOTHESES**

#### How would a team distinguish them?

##### A diagnostic sequence

1. Freeze identity. Record device, software, waveform, calibration graph, clock, and environmental snapshot.

2. Measure before. Run a short spectroscopy or canary that directly estimates coupler frequency and gate behavior.

3. Run the long sequence. Preserve shot order and timestamps instead of only an average.

4. Interleave monitors. Re-measure the coupler or a sensitive proxy throughout the run.

6. Compare time series. Ask whether the device moved, the selected calibration was old, or both.

03. · TECHNOLOGY

#### What a D-Wave annealer actually does

The correct mental model is a programmable physical sampler for low-energy states of an Ising model—not a faster CPU and not a gate circuit.

| 1 · FORMULATE | 2 · MAP | 3 · ANNEAL | 4 · SAMPLE | 5 · VALIDATE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Express the objective<br>and constraints as a<br>binary quadratic model<br>(QUBO/BQM). | Map logical variables<br>onto connected physical<br>qubits; chains may<br>represent one logical<br>variable. | Evolve from an easy<br>initial Hamiltonian<br>toward the problem<br>Hamiltonian. | Read many stochastic<br>low-energy samples,<br>often with gauges and<br>repeated calls. | Unembed, repair<br>constraints, compare<br>solution quality and<br>measure total workflow<br>cost. |

##### The objective

A common binary quadratic form is E(x) = Σᵢ aᵢxᵢ + Σᵢ<j bᵢⱼxᵢxⱼ, with xᵢ ∈ {0,1}. The annealer samples candidate assignments that tend toward lower energy. Constraints are encoded as penalties or handled by hybrid orchestration. A useful answer is therefore not guaranteed by one read; it emerges from formulation, parameter selection, repeated sampling and classical checking.

| Zephyr | Advantage2 | 20-way connectivity | Further reduces embedding cost and supports denser logical<br>structures. [2,3] |

- Model construction, constraint handling and scaling coefficients.

- Minor embedding, chain-strength selection and calibration-aware parameter choices.

##### The field learned a better question

Early discussion often asked whether a D-Wave processor was ‘quantum.’ Later work separated at least four questions: (1) Are quantum states/resources present? (2) Does the machine reproduce a target quantum model? (3) Does time-to-solution scale better than a fair classical comparator? (4) Does the complete workflow produce enough value to matter? A yes at one level does not imply a yes at the next.

DURABLE CONTRIBUTION Even inconclusive speedup studies improved benchmark design: instance selection, scaling ranges, success probabilities, tuning budgets, parallelism and preprocessing must be disclosed. D-Wave hardware helped turn ‘quantum speedup’ from a slogan into a measurement discipline.

06. · EVIDENCE AND DEBATE

#### How to read the 2025 ‘quantum supremacy’ result

The paper is peer reviewed. The broadest publicity language is still an interpretation, and the classical-comparison frontier continues to move.

##### What the paper did

- Studied nonequilibrium magnetic spin dynamics on several graph families, including square, cubic, diamond and biclique structures. [9]

- Benchmarked against classical methods in regimes where cross-checks were feasible, then analyzed scaling into harder regimes.

- Used the annealer as a programmable quantum simulator rather than merely as a black-box business optimizer.

##### What D-Wave claimed

- D-Wave described the result as ‘quantum supremacy on a useful problem’ and publicized an estimate of roughly 20 minutes on its system versus nearly one million years for a specific classical simulation on Frontier. This is a company-framed extrapolation tied to particular methods and workloads, not a general statement about computers. [9,10]

##### Why the debate continues

| Question | Why it is difficult | Good scientific posture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best classical baseline? | Tensor networks, Monte Carlo, GPUs and<br>problem-specific approximations improve after a<br>quantum result is published. | Specify algorithm, hardware, accuracy target,<br>tuning effort and release code/data. |
| Equivalent output? | A fast approximation and a high-fidelity dynamical<br>sample may answer different questions. | Compare accuracy and observables, not wall-clock<br>time alone. |
| Generalization? | Hardness on a spin model does not imply advantage<br>for scheduling, logistics or arbitrary optimization. | State the graph family, parameter regime and task<br>boundary. |
| End-to-end cost? | Programming, annealing, readout and network time<br>are only part of formulation and validation. | Report both quantum-processing and total<br>workflow measures. |

That response is relevant evidence, but it is not an independent resolution. The correct conclusion is specific: a strong peer-reviewed quantum-simulation result exists; the breadth and durability of its classical separation remain under scrutiny. [9,10]

##### Why dual-rail qubits are relevant

Quantum Circuits’ gate-model approach encodes information across two physical modes so that certain loss events can be detected rather than silently becoming logical errors. In principle this changes the engineering tradeoff between fidelity, error detection and error correction. In practice, the acquisition should be evaluated through future published device metrics—not by assuming that architectural promise equals a delivered fault-tolerant computer. [1]

##### Likely contribution of the New Haven systems-software team

- Translate experimental protocols into versioned, testable control and calibration workflows.

- Build observability: telemetry, provenance, error taxonomies, profiler traces and reproducible experiment records.

- Protect physicists from brittle infrastructure without hiding the physics that determines correctness.

- Automate system operations while preserving human approval for unsafe or irreversible actions.

- Create APIs and back-end services that can evolve with hardware generations and experimental uncertainty.

THE ROLE’S STRATEGIC IMPORTANCE At this stage, software is not a wrapper around finished hardware. Calibration, experiment control, debugging and automation are part of the scientific instrument itself. Reliability determines how quickly the team can learn—and whether a research result becomes a product capability.

11. · GLOSSARY AND SYNTHESIS

#### Terms that keep the discussion precise

Precision is especially valuable in quantum computing because one broad word can hide several different claims.

| Term | Working definition |
| --- | --- |
| Quantum annealing | Analog evolution designed to sample low-energy states of an encoded optimization or spin-model<br>Hamiltonian. |
| QUBO / BQM | Binary quadratic model: linear and pairwise coefficients over binary variables; a common application<br>interface to annealers. |
| Ising model | Equivalent spin representation using variables in {−1,+1}; native language for many magnetic and<br>optimization problems. |
| Minor embedding | Mapping a logical problem graph onto hardware connectivity, often representing one logical variable<br>with a chain of qubits. |
| Quantum processing time | Time attributed to programming, annealing and readout on the QPU; not necessarily the full<br>application latency. |
| Time to solution | Expected time to reach a target success probability, accounting for repeated probabilistic samples and<br>sometimes tuning. |
| Quantum advantage | A demonstrated benefit over a specified classical method for a specified task and metric. It is not<br>automatically general. |
| Quantum supremacy | A threshold claim that a quantum device performs a defined computation infeasible for known classical<br>methods; usefulness and generality are separate questions. |
| Hybrid solver | An algorithm that combines classical search/decomposition with calls to a quantum sampler. |
| Dual-rail qubit | A gate-model encoding across two modes that can expose certain loss errors as detectable events. |

##### Synthesis

D-Wave’s history is a case study in how a controversial architecture can become scientifically productive through persistence, instrumentation and access. The company forced hard questions about what counts as quantum behavior, fair benchmarking and useful advantage. It also demonstrated that commercialization requires far more than qubits: calibration, APIs, cloud operations, developer tools, services and a willingness to compare against classical methods. Its next chapter will be judged by whether it can preserve that operating discipline while advancing both annealing and gate-model hardware.

ONE-SENTENCE TAKEAWAY D-Wave’s enduring contribution is turning quantum annealing into a large-scale, accessible experimental and commercial platform; its enduring challenge is proving where that platform changes real outcomes better than continuously improving classical computation.
