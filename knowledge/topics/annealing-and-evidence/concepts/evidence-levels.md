## Plain-language meaning

Evidence levels distinguish what kind of source supports a claim and how far that support extends.

## Categories used by the company study

- **Peer reviewed:** supports bounded methods and scientific conclusions.
- **Regulatory:** supports corporate history, products, risks, and business disclosures under filing obligations.
- **Partner reported:** supports that a partner ran a program or deployment.
- **Company reported:** supports what the company claims about its own products or cases.
- **Forward looking:** describes plans or targets, not delivered results.

## Why it matters

A source can be appropriate without independently validating every implication. A product page can establish a specification; it cannot by itself prove practical advantage over the best classical method.

## Claim ladder

1. Quantum behavior is present.
2. The device reproduces a target quantum model.
3. A fair metric beats a specified classical comparator.
4. The complete workflow changes a practical outcome.

A yes at one level does not imply the next.

## Self-check

1. Which source supports a company's own product specification?
2. Why does peer review not make a result universal?
3. Which claim level includes the complete workflow?
